# Phase 3: Cross-Sector Domain Generalization Report

## 1. The Challenge of Cross-Sector Generalization
In the Hack4Hack 2026 competition:
- **Training stars (18,000)** and **test stars (7,000)** originate from **different observing sectors** of a TESS-like space telescope.
- Each sector experiences different pointing orientations, momentum dump thruster rhythms, scattered light levels from the Earth/Moon, and camera thermal gradients.
- Models that memorize training sector quirks (such as raw cadence dump numbers or camera IDs) suffer severe performance drops on the private leaderboard.

---

## 2. Quantitative Train vs. Test Distribution Comparison
Based on two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov ($\text{KS}$) tests and distribution audits across all 85 features:

### A. Perfectly Invariant Features ($\text{KS} < 0.05$)
1. **Missing Data Fraction (`nan_frac`)**: Median is exactly $0.0403$ (4.03%) in both sectors ($\text{KS} = 0.0000, p = 1.0$), capturing the scheduled mid-sector data downlink gap.
2. **Stellar Population Parameters**:
   - $T_{\text{mag}}$ ($\text{KS} = 0.0102, p = 0.665$)
   - $T_{\text{eff}}$ ($\text{KS} = 0.0069, p = 0.970$)
   - $\log g$ ($\text{KS} = 0.0072, p = 0.953$)
   - Radius ($\text{KS} = 0.0070, p = 0.962$)
   - Contamination ($\text{KS} = 0.0089, p = 0.819$)
   *Conclusion*: The stellar population surveyed in the test sector is structurally identical in astrophysics to the training sector.

### B. Shifted Instrumental Features ($\text{KS} > 0.15$)
1. **Raw Flux Upper Percentiles (`p99`, `p95`)**: Test sector has systematically higher positive tail values due to elevated high-frequency scattered light.
2. **Raw Cadence Quality Flags**: Because the test sector experienced a different thruster firing rhythm, raw dump counters shift between sectors.

---

## 3. Implemented Domain Adaptation Strategies

### Strategy 1: Complete Elimination of Camera/CCD Shortcuts
- `camera` and `ccd` columns are **strictly excluded** from our feature matrices.
- Training models on camera IDs would allow the model to memorize sector-specific sensor trends that do not transfer to the test sector.

### Strategy 2: 2D Rolling Median Detrending
- A fast 24.5-hour rolling median filter along time (`axis=1`) is subtracted from each star's normalized light curve individually.
- This removes telescope thermal drift, gradual scattered light ramps, and stellar rotation before transit dip detection.

### Strategy 3: Local Scale-Invariant Centroid Vetting
- Rather than relying on absolute millipixel displacements, centroid shift during dips is normalized by the star's own baseline pointing scatter:
  $$\text{cen\_shift\_normalized} = \frac{\text{cen\_shift\_in\_dip}}{\text{disp\_std} + 1e-4}$$
  $$\text{cen\_shift\_ratio} = \frac{\text{mean\_disp\_in\_dip}}{\text{mean\_disp\_out\_dip} + 1e-8}$$
- This makes background binary vetting robust against higher pointing jitter in the test sector.

### Strategy 4: Relative Spread Scaling
- High percentiles are scaled relative to the star's interquartile range:
  $$\text{p99\_norm} = \frac{p99 - p75}{\text{IQR} + 1e-8}, \quad \text{dip\_to\_peak\_ratio} = \frac{p25 - p01}{p99 - p75 + 1e-8}$$
- This cancels out sector-wide photon noise floor shifts.

### Strategy 5: 5-Fold Probability Averaging
- Predictions on the test set are generated by averaging the calibrated probability distributions of 5 independently trained cross-validation models.
- Bagging reduces model variance and prevents any single split's boundary quirks from biasing test predictions.
